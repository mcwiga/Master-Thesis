\chapter{Classification of Lagrangian Fibrations over Closed Surfaces}

Now that we have classified the integral affine structures on closed surfaces,
we can look to classify the Lagrangian fibrations $E \to B$ over these
surfaces. The integral affine structure on the base $B$ is one invariant of a
Lagrangian fibration however it is not the only one. We have seen that there is
a correspondence between integral affine structures on $B$ and full rank
lattices in $T^*B$ locally spanned by closed one forms. This lattice $\Lambda
    \subset T^*B$ gives rise to a Lagrangian fibration $\pi : T^*B/\Lambda \to B$
which is a canonical Lagrangian fibration we can associate to $B$ once we have
fixed the integral affine structure on $B$. There are in general, other
Lagrangian fibrations over $B$ which are in some sense \emph{twistings} of this
canonical fibration.

We wish to classify these fibrations up to fibrewise symplectomorphism,
covering the identity. We shall see that there are obstructions both to a
Lagrangian bundle over $B$ being topologically equivalent to this canonical
Lagrangian fibration $\pi : T^*B/\Lambda \to B$ as well as obstructions to a
Lagrangian bundle over $B$ being symplectically equivalent to the canonical
Lagrangian bundle. Since the problem of classifying these bundles will be an
issue of stitching together locally defined Lagrangian fibrations into a global
one, it will be helpful to do this in the language of sheafs. (TODO: this is

\subsection{Introducing some sheafs}
We shall make use of the following sheafs on $B$.
\begin{definition}
    Let $B$ be a closed integral affine surface with period lattice $\Lambda \subset T^*B$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\mathcal{P}$ is the sheaf of sections of the period lattice $\Lambda \subset T^*B$.
        \item $\Lambda \mathcal{T}^*B$ is the sheaf of Lagrangian sections of $T^*B$ and $\mathcal{T}^*B$ is the sheaf of all sections of $T^*B$.
        \item $\Lambda \mathcal{T}^*B/\mathcal{P}$ is the sheaf of Lagrangian sections of $T^*B/\Lambda$ and $\mathcal{T}^*B/\mathcal{P}$ is the sheaf of all
              sections of $T^*B/\Lambda$.
        \item $\Lambda^2 B$ is the sheaf of closed $2$-forms on $B$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark} \label{rem:differential-descends}
    Let $\Omega^1(B)$ denote the sheaf of differential forms on $B$. Then the differential
    $$
        d :\Omega^1(B) \to \Lambda^2(B)
    $$
    descends to a well-defined differential
    $$
        d_{\mathcal{P}}: \mathcal{T}^*B/\mathcal{P} \to \Lambda^2(B)
    $$
    on the sections of $T^*B/\Lambda$ as sections of $\Lambda$ are closed and hence vanish under this differential.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
    The following diagram of sheaves is exact in all rows and columns.
    \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
            & & 0 \arrow[d] & 0 \arrow[d] & \\
            0 \arrow[r] & \mathcal{P} \arrow[r, "i"] \arrow[d, equal]
            & \Lambda \mathcal{T}^*B \arrow[r, "\mathrm{pr}"] \arrow[d]
            & \Lambda \mathcal{T}^*B / \mathcal{P} \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "j"]
            & 0 \\
            0 \arrow[r] & \mathcal{P} \arrow[r]
            & \mathcal{T}^*B \arrow[r, "\mathrm{pr}"] \arrow[d, "d"]
            & \mathcal{T}^*B / \mathcal{P} \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "d"]
            & 0 \\
            & & \Lambda^2 B \arrow[r, equal] \arrow[d]
            & \Lambda^2 B \arrow[d] \\
            & & 0 & 0
        \end{tikzcd}
    \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    It is clear the the two horizontal sequences given by the inclusion and projection maps are exact. We show that
    $$
        0 \to \Lambda \mathcal{T}^*B \to \mathcal{T}^*B \to \Lambda^2 B \to 0
    $$
    is exact. The map $\mathcal{T}^*B \to \mathcal{T}^*B$ is the inclusion map and so is injective. It is also clear that the differential
    map $d$ is surjective onto the space of closed $2$-forms by definition. The kernel of the differential $d$ is the closed $1$-forms on $T^*B$ but these are
    given by Lagrangian sections. The other vertical sequence follows by the same argument and Remark \ref{rem:differential-descends}.
\end{proof}
